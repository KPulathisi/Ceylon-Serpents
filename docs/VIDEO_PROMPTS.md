# Movement video prompts

These are four prompts for AI video generators (Google Veo, Sora, Kling, Runway and similar). Each one shows a single locomotion type with a real Sri Lankan species that is known to use it. Upload the results in the admin panel under **Movement videos**. Keep "AI-generated" ticked so the site labels them.

**Recommended output:** 16:9, 1280×720 or 1920×1080, 6–8 seconds, no audio, seamless loop, MP4 (H.264) under 10 MB.

**Add this to every prompt as a negative prompt, or append it:**
> no people, no hands, no text, no logos, no captions, no extra heads, no legs, no limbs, no fantasy features, no unnatural glow, no exaggerated speed, no aggressive striking, anatomically accurate snake, realistic scale patterns

---

## 1. Lateral undulation: checkered keelback (*Fowlea piscator*)

> Wildlife documentary footage, Sri Lanka. A checkered keelback water snake (Fowlea piscator), olive-yellow with rows of black checkerboard spots and keeled scales, swims slowly across the still, shallow water of a rural paddy field at the edge of a village tank, with lotus leaves and young rice plants around it. Its body forms smooth, regular S-shaped waves that travel from head to tail, pushing against the water and leaving gentle ripples behind each curve. Then it glides out onto the wet, muddy bund, keeping the same S-shaped side-to-side motion against small clumps of grass. Camera: slightly elevated side angle, slow lateral tracking shot that keeps the whole body in frame, shallow depth of field. Soft early-morning light with mist over the paddy. Natural colours, calm and educational, realistic speed. Seamless loop.

## 2. Rectilinear movement: Indian python (*Python molurus*)

> Wildlife documentary footage, dry-zone forest floor in Sri Lanka, similar to Wilpattu National Park. A large Indian python (Python molurus), pale tan with big dark-brown blotches outlined in black and an arrowhead-shaped mark on its head, crawls forward in a perfectly straight line across reddish sandy soil scattered with dry leaves. The body stays almost straight, with no side-to-side curves. Close to the ground, waves of belly scales visibly lift, slide forward and anchor again in sections, moving from head to tail like a caterpillar, so the snake creeps forward slowly and smoothly. Camera: low ground-level side view, close-up following the middle of the body, then easing back to show the straight-line path. Warm, golden late-afternoon light filtering through thorny scrub. Realistic slow pace, calm educational tone. Seamless loop.

## 3. Concertina movement: Indian rat snake (*Ptyas mucosa*)

> Wildlife documentary footage in a Sri Lankan home garden. An Indian rat snake (Ptyas mucosa), long and slender, olive-brown with dark crossbars towards the tail and large dark eyes, climbs the rough, ringed trunk of a coconut palm. It uses concertina movement: the rear of its body bunches into tight bends that grip the bark, the front of the body stretches upward and finds a new grip, then the rear coils are drawn up behind it, repeating like a concertina folding and unfolding. Banana leaves and a whitewashed garden wall are softly out of focus in the background. Camera: static medium shot from the side with the trunk running diagonally through the frame, so each grip-and-extend cycle is clearly visible. Bright, soft daylight with dappled shade. Realistic speed, calm educational tone. Seamless loop.

## 4. Sidewinding: saw-scaled viper (*Echis carinatus*)

> Wildlife documentary footage on loose coastal sand dunes near Mannar in north-western Sri Lanka, with sparse low scrub and a pale sky. A small saw-scaled viper (Echis carinatus), sandy brown with pale wavy zigzag markings along its sides, darker blotches on its back and a pear-shaped head, moves across the soft sand by sidewinding. It lifts sections of its body clear of the surface and places them down slightly ahead, so it travels diagonally while only two short parts of the body touch the sand at any moment. It leaves a series of separate, parallel J-shaped tracks behind it. Camera: elevated three-quarter view at about 45 degrees, holding steady so both the moving snake and the growing line of tracks are visible. Low, warm late-afternoon sun casting long shadows that make the tracks stand out. Realistic speed, calm educational tone. Seamless loop.

Regenerate any clip that misleads. On the site, these videos are labelled as AI-generated illustrations.
